## What "Dedicated Marketer" Actually Means

A dedicated marketer is one person, working full-time (or a fixed committed schedule) on your business only. Not a shared agency pod. Not a freelancer juggling six clients. One brain, fully loaded with your context — your product, your customers, your numbers.

That last part matters more than people admit. Marketing compounds when the person running it remembers why last quarter's campaign failed. Agencies reset that memory every time your account manager changes.

The math most businesses miss: a $5K/month agency retainer is $60K/year — comparable to a marketer's salary — but 30–50% of a typical agency fee funds overhead: account managers, office costs, sales commissions, margin. With an embedded model, that markup layer is removed. You pay what the expert earns, plus a coordination fee you can see.

## When Each Model Wins

**Hire in-house if:** you have $90K–$150K/year for a seasoned specialist, 2–3 months for recruiting, and enough ongoing work to keep them busy for years. Best for companies past ~$5M revenue building a long-term team.

**Use an agency if:** you need a one-off specialized project — a rebrand, a site launch — where a multi-skill team for 8 weeks beats one person for a year.

**Use a freelancer if:** your need is under ~15 hours/week and you can manage them yourself.

**Use an embedded dedicated marketer if:** you need full-time output and accumulated context but can't justify (or can't wait for) an in-house hiring cycle. This is the middle path: in-house dedication, agency-fast start, no markup.

## How to Hire a Dedicated Marketer in 7 Days (Step-by-Step)

1. **Write the outcome, not the job description.** "Take clinic revenue from X to Y in 90 days via paid + CRO" beats a list of 14 tools.
2. **Pick the specialism.** Performance marketer, SEO/content lead, or full-stack growth generalist — one primary skill, not all three.
3. **Vet on past numbers.** Ask for baseline → action → measured result on a real campaign. Genuine experts walk through this fluently; pretenders talk in adjectives.
4. **Run a paid working session.** One week or one sprint. Watch how they think with your actual data.
5. **Set a 30-day scorecard.** 2–3 metrics, reviewed weekly.
6. **Demand a replacement clause.** If the fit is wrong, you shouldn't restart recruiting. (AnantaSutra replaces within 48 hours.)

## The Cost Trap Nobody Mentions

Recruitment agencies charge an estimated 20–30% of base salary just to *find* you a marketer — $20K–$30K on a $100K role — before they've done a day of marketing. Marketing agencies bake similar economics into retainers. Freelance platforms take their cut too. Every model has a middle layer; the only question is whether you can see it. Pick the model where the fee is transparent and the markup is small.

In the US, in-house averages ~$85K/year (entry ~$45K, senior $90K–$150K) plus benefits. Agencies run $1,500–$5,000+/month for foundational scopes. For ongoing, compounding work (funnels, SEO, lifecycle) — usually yes, because context accumulates in one accountable person. For short specialized projects, an agency's multi-skill team can be the better fit.
