## Why businesses go looking for an agency alternative

I've sat on both sides of this table. The pattern that pushes founders to search "agency alternative" is almost always the same three complaints:

1. **You're one of 15–20 accounts.** Your work gets whatever hours are left after the bigger retainers are served.
2. **You're paying for overhead, not output.** Account managers, office costs and agency margin sit between your money and the person actually doing the work. Industry comparisons routinely note agency retainers carry significant markup over the delivery talent's cost.
3. **You have no idea who's doing the work.** The senior person pitches; a junior executes. You can't interview them, can't manage them, can't keep them.

None of this means agencies are bad. It means the agency model is built for campaigns and projects — not for a business that needs consistent, compounding work month after month.

## The four agency alternatives, compared

There are really only four ways to get marketing or development capacity without a traditional agency retainer:

| Model | Who does the work | Typical cost structure | Dedication | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Freelancer** | Independent contractor | Hourly / per project | Split across clients | Ghosting, no backup |
| **In-house hire** | Your employee | Salary + benefits + hiring cost | 100% yours | Slow to hire (months), hard to replace |
| **Fractional expert** | Senior specialist, 10–20 hrs/week | Monthly retainer | Partial | Limited hours, strategy > execution |
| **Embedded expert** | Full-time vetted pro inside your team | Salary + small transparent fee | 100% yours | Low — replacement guarantee |

A freelancer is cheap to start but splits attention across clients. An in-house hire gives you full control but takes months to recruit and is painful to replace if it doesn't work out. Fractional experts (the MarketerHire / Growth Collective style model) give you senior brains for 10–20 hours a week — good for strategy, thin for execution.

The embedded model is the only one that combines full-time dedication, fast start, and an exit ramp if the person isn't right.

## When an agency is still the right call

Being straight about the limits:

- **One-off projects** (a rebrand, a launch film) — hire an agency or studio. Embedding a full-timer for a 6-week project makes no sense.
- **You need 6 specialists for 2 hours each** — an agency's shared bench genuinely fits that shape.
- **You have zero internal direction** and want outcomes fully owned externally — a managed service fits better than an embedded hire you'd need to point somewhere.

If your need is **ongoing, execution-heavy, and central to revenue** — ads, SEO, product development, content — the embedded model wins on cost per productive hour, speed and accountability.
