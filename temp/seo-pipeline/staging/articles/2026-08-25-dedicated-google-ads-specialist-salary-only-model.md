## What a dedicated Google Ads specialist actually is

A dedicated Google Ads specialist is one person, working full-time, only on your accounts. Not a pod. Not a shared account manager juggling 10–15 clients. One certified PPC professional who joins your standups, uses your Slack, and treats your ad budget like it's their own money — because their job depends on it.

Under the **salary-only model**, the commercial structure changes too:

- You pay the specialist's actual market salary plus a small, transparent placement fee.
- There is no percentage-of-ad-spend fee, no retainer markup, no "management fee" that scales with your budget.
- The specialist is exclusively yours for the engagement — their calendar has one client on it.

That last point is the whole game. Google Ads performance compounds through daily attention: search-term mining, negative keyword hygiene, budget pacing, creative testing in Performance Max, conversion-tracking fixes. An agency account manager splitting attention across a dozen clients physically cannot give your account that.

## Why the salary-only model beats percentage-of-spend

Percentage-of-spend pricing creates a quiet conflict of interest: the agency earns more when you spend more, whether or not the spend is efficient. Industry-standard management fees run 10–20% of budget (dropping to 5–10% only above ~€50K/month spend).

Salary-only removes that incentive entirely. Your specialist's pay doesn't move when your budget does. The only way they win is by making the account perform — which is exactly how you win.

## What to demand from a dedicated Google Ads specialist in 2026

The skill bar moved. Search-only competence is no longer enough. Vet for:

1. **Performance Max and Demand Gen** — structure, asset-group strategy, and knowing when *not* to use PMax.
2. **Conversion tracking + GA4** — server-side tagging awareness, enhanced conversions, offline conversion imports. Most "underperforming" accounts are actually measurement problems.
3. **Smart Bidding fluency** — tROAS/tCPA setup, when to use portfolio strategies, seasonality adjustments.
4. **Shopping feed management** if you're e-commerce.
5. **A live Skillshop certification and real account screenshots** — published hiring guides consistently recommend both before you commit.

A dedicated specialist should also produce a weekly one-page report: spend, conversions, CPA/ROAS, what changed, what's next. If they can't explain the account in plain language, they don't understand it.

## Freelancer vs dedicated specialist: the honest comparison

Freelancers genuinely win in one scenario: a small, well-defined account (roughly $2K–$5K/month spend) where you can direct the work yourself. Published analyses agree the freelancer model delivers strong skill-per-dollar in that band.

The dedicated model wins when:

- Ad spend and complexity justify daily, not weekly, attention.
- You don't want to be the project manager, QA department, and strategist.
- Continuity matters — a 48-hour replacement guarantee beats a freelancer's two-week holiday with your campaigns on autopilot.
- You want the specialist inside your team's context: sales feedback loops, promo calendars, landing-page iterations.
