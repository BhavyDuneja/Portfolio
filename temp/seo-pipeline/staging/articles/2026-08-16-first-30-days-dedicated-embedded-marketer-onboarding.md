## Why the first 30 days decide everything

An embedded marketer is not an agency. There's no account manager buffering you, no shared pod splitting attention across 12 clients. You get one person, full-time, inside your Slack and your standups. That's the whole advantage — but it means *you* set the direction, and the first 30 days is when direction gets set or lost.

The industry consensus backs this up: structured 30-60-90 day plans exist precisely because the first 30 days are for absorbing context — team, tools, processes — before independent execution kicks in. Remote and augmented staff who go through real onboarding "ramp faster, ask fewer redundant questions, and produce better work sooner." Winging it is the fastest way to fail.

Here's the week-by-week plan we hand clients on day zero.

## Week 1: Access, context, and one shipped task

**Days 1–2: Kill the access bottleneck.** Before your marketer's first call, have ready:

- Email, Slack/Teams, and calendar access
- Ad accounts (Meta, Google), analytics (GA4, Search Console), CMS, social profiles
- Brand assets folder — logos, past creatives, tone-of-voice notes, anything
- One document answering: what do we sell, to whom, at what price, and what's worked before

Nothing slows an embedded hire more than being outside the information flow. They don't need access to everything — they need access to everything that touches their work.

**Days 2–3: Four to six short intro calls.** Fifteen minutes each with the founder, sales lead, product owner, and whoever handled marketing before. Not one two-hour meeting — short, separate calls. The marketer's job in these calls: learn what customers actually say, not what the deck says.

**Days 4–7: Ship one small, real task.** Not a test. A real deliverable: rewrite the homepage hero, fix the Google Business Profile, draft a week of social posts, audit one ad campaign. A quick win in week one does two things — proves the pipe works end-to-end (brief → work → review → live), and builds trust on both sides early.

When we placed a dedicated marketer with Awish Clinic, the week-1 task was auditing their existing lead flow. That audit became the roadmap that took them from Rs 25L to Rs 40L in monthly revenue within 60 days. The audit itself took four days.

## Week 2: Full channel audit + 90-day plan draft

Now the marketer knows your business well enough to be useful strategically.

**The audit.** Every channel gets a one-page verdict: what's running, what it costs, what it returns, keep/kill/fix. Website, SEO, paid ads, social, email, listings. Numbers where they exist, honest "no data" where they don't.

**The 90-day plan draft.** By day 14, you should see a one-page plan: 2–3 priority channels, expected outputs per week, and what success looks like at day 90. You review it, cut it down, approve it. This is the document you'll manage against — without it, "how's marketing going?" has no answer.

**The cadence gets set.** Lock the operating rhythm now: a daily async check-in (5 lines in Slack), one 30-minute weekly call, and a simple tracker (sheet or Notion) both sides can see. This is the entire management overhead of a well-onboarded embedded marketer — roughly 2 hours a week of your time.

## Week 3: First real campaign live

Week 3 is where embedded beats agency visibly. An agency is still in "strategy phase" at day 18. Your embedded marketer should be launching.

Pick the highest-confidence play from the audit — usually one of:

- Relaunching or restructuring one paid campaign with proper tracking
- A landing page + lead form for the core offer
- A local SEO push (for clinics, real estate, local services)
- An outbound content series if the sales cycle is long

One campaign, done properly, with tracking that works. Not five campaigns at 20% each. When Zoom Wheels got their embedded marketer focused on one funnel instead of scattered posting, engagement rose 46%. Focus is the multiplier.

**Also in week 3:** the marketer should now be attending your regular team meetings as a team member, not a guest. Embedded means embedded.

## Week 4: Baselines, KPIs, and the 30-day review

**Lock the baselines.** Whatever the numbers are — leads/month, cost per lead, traffic, conversion rate — write them down now. You cannot judge day-90 performance without a day-30 baseline. (Be honest here: if you don't have Search Console or CRM data, the baseline is "unknown," and fixing that is itself a KPI.)

**The 30-day review — 45 minutes, three questions:**

1. **Output:** What shipped in 30 days? (There should be a real list.)
2. **Fit:** Does this person communicate well, push back when needed, and operate without hand-holding?
3. **Plan:** Is the 90-day plan still right, or did the audit change it?

If the answer to question 1 is "not much" and you followed weeks 1–3, that's a talent problem — and it's exactly why AnantaSutra has a 48-hour replacement guarantee. If you *didn't* follow weeks 1–3, fix the onboarding before blaming the person.

## FAQ

**What should an embedded marketer accomplish in the first 30 days?**
Ship one real task in week 1, deliver a full channel audit and 90-day plan by day 14, launch one properly tracked campaign by day 21, and lock baseline KPIs by day 30.

**How long before a dedicated marketer shows results?**
Output starts in week 1; measurable business results typically show at 60–90 days. Awish Clinic went from Rs 25L to Rs 40L monthly revenue in 60 days — but the week-1 audit is what made it possible.

**How much of my time does onboarding an embedded marketer take?**
Front-loaded: 4–5 hours in week 1 (calls + context doc), then about 2 hours per week (daily async check-in + one weekly call).

**What if the marketer isn't working out after 30 days?**

**Is an embedded marketer better than an agency for the first 90 days?**
For execution speed, yes — an embedded expert ships in week 1 while agencies are typically still in strategy phase. Agencies suit businesses that want zero management involvement and accept the 30–50% markup for it.

**Do I need a 30-60-90 day plan or is 30 days enough?**
Start with 30. The day-14 deliverable in this plan *is* the 90-day plan — built from your real data, not a template.
