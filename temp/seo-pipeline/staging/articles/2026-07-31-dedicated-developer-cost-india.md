## What "dedicated" must mean (or you're overpaying for a word)

A dedicated developer is full-time on your codebase, in your standups, on your Slack/WhatsApp, working under your priorities. It is not a shared "resource" splitting sprints across three clients, and not a freelancer fitting you in around bigger gigs. Checklist worth contract language: named person (interview them before they start — at AnantaSutra you pick from 2–3 vetted profiles), your tools and your repo, daily availability in your hours, and a replacement path (48 hours in our model) if the fit is wrong.

## Cost for overseas businesses (the arbitrage, honestly stated)

For a UK, US or Gulf business, a senior Indian developer at ₹1.5–2.5L/month (≈ $1,800–3,000 / AED 6,600–11,000, est.) compares against $8,000–15,000+ for an equivalent local hire — before visa, insurance and severance costs. That's why GCC SMEs increasingly run embedded Indian developers inside their teams remotely: same standups, same accountability, one-third the cost. The catch to manage is accountability, not capability — which is exactly what vetting, weekly reviews and replacement guarantees exist for.

## The five questions that set your real budget

1. **Junior, mid or senior?** Most SMEs need one strong mid-level, not a cheap junior or an idle senior.
2. **Project or continuous?** A 6-week build suits a project quote; a product that ships monthly needs a dedicated person.
3. **Who vets?** If you can't technically interview, the vetting layer (partner or platform) is worth paying a *visible* fee for — not an invisible 50% markup.
4. **Who replaces?** Ask every vendor their replacement SLA in hours, in writing.
5. **What happens when you pause?** No-lock-in models let you scale down between releases; agencies usually don't.
