## What "hire dedicated developer India" actually costs in 2026

Public benchmarks from Indian dev-shop pricing guides (Bacancy, SolGuruz, InnovationM, Dotsquares and others) cluster like this. These are **estimates from published vendor guides, not verified salary data** — real rates depend on stack, city, and seniority:

| Level | Hourly (vendor-quoted) | Monthly (vendor-quoted) |
|---|---|---|
| Junior developer | $15–$25/hr | ~$1,500–$2,500/mo |
| Mid-level developer | $30–$50/hr | $2,500–$5,500/mo |
| Senior / specialist (AI/ML, DevOps) | $60–$80/hr | $5,000–$8,000/mo |

Now the part vendors don't put in the table: a strong mid-level developer in India earns roughly ₹10–20 lakh a year (~$1,000–$2,000/month) in salary. When you pay a platform $4,500/month for that person, the difference is markup — sales commissions, account managers, bench costs, office overhead. Often 50–100% on top of the salary.

That markup buys you convenience. The question is whether it buys you $2,000+/month of convenience, every month, forever.

## What "dedicated" should actually mean

Most vendors use "dedicated" to mean "billed full-time to you." That's the floor, not the standard. When you hire a dedicated developer through an embedded model, insist on:

- **Exclusivity** — one developer, one client. No shared benches.
- **Your tools, your process** — the developer works in your repo, your ticketing, your CI. Not a black-box delivery process.
- **Direct communication** — you talk to the developer, not an account manager relaying summaries.
- **A replacement clause** — if it's not working, a defined swap window. Ours is 48 hours.
- **Salary transparency** — you should know what the developer is actually paid. If a vendor won't tell you, the markup is the reason.

## Red flags when hiring Indian developers

- **Rates that look too good** ($9/hr "dedicated" developers) usually mean juniors supervised in bulk, or time-split across clients.
- **No interview before assignment.** If you can't interview the actual developer, you're buying a lottery ticket.
- **Hourly billing for a "dedicated" resource.** Full-time exclusivity should be a flat monthly number. Hourly framing invites padding.
- **Vague replacement terms.** "We'll work with you" is not a policy. Get a defined window in writing.
- **The account-manager wall.** If every conversation routes through a non-technical intermediary, integration will be shallow.

## When each model genuinely wins

No fake balance here — but honest boundaries:

- **Freelancer:** a small, well-scoped task (a landing page, a script, a prototype) where you have technical oversight. Days of work, not months.
- **Project agency:** you have zero technical capacity in-house and want to hand over an entire build with someone else accountable for delivery.
- **Embedded dedicated developer:** you have ongoing product work, someone (even semi-technical) to set direction, and you want the economics of an in-house hire without running Indian payroll, compliance, and sourcing yourself. This is where the salary-only model wins on cost by a wide margin.
