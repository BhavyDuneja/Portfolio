## Why SMEs quit agencies in the first place

I'll be blunt about the pattern, because we see it every week. An SME signs a Rs50,000–1,50,000/month retainer. The pitch team disappears after signing. The actual work goes to a junior handling 10–15 accounts. Reports arrive full of impressions and "brand lift", and when you ask "how many customers did this bring?", the answer gets vague.

The structural problem is markup and attention-splitting: agencies typically layer 30–50% on top of the cost of the people doing the work, and no one on the account thinks about your business for more than a couple of hours a week. That's not an ethics problem, it's a business-model problem. You fix it by changing the model, not the vendor.

### 1. Freelancers — cheapest entry, highest variance

Good freelancers exist, and for a scoped job (a landing page, a logo, one campaign setup) they're often the right call. The problems start when you try to run *ongoing* marketing on freelancers: they juggle multiple clients, priorities shift, and there's no institutional backup if they go quiet mid-month. Coordinating three freelancers across content, ads and design becomes your unpaid second job.

### 2. In-house hire — maximum control, maximum commitment

An in-house marketer learns your business deeply and compounds context. But for most SMEs the math is hard: hiring takes 40+ days on average, a wrong hire costs months, and salary + benefits + tools + management is a bigger number than the salary line suggests. If you can afford it and can *manage* it, do it. Many SMEs can't do either yet.

### 3. Fractional marketer — strategy without hands

A fractional CMO gives you senior thinking a few days a month: positioning, channel plan, budget allocation. Genuinely valuable — and genuinely incomplete. Strategy decks don't run ads, write pages, or answer leads. Fractional works best *paired with* execution capacity you already have.

### 4. AI and DIY tools — cheap software, expensive founder time

AI tools in 2026 can draft content, build simple ads and automate reporting at a fraction of agency cost. For a founder with time and channel instincts, this is a real option. But the tool doesn't decide what to say, to whom, or why — and every hour you spend running campaigns is an hour not spent running the business. Software is a multiplier on marketing skill; multiplied by zero, it stays zero.

A: AI/DIY tools have the lowest cash cost (often under Rs25k/month in software), but they consume founder time. The cheapest *managed* option for ongoing work is usually an embedded dedicated expert, because you pay a salary instead of a salary-plus-30–50%-markup retainer.

**Q: What is an embedded marketing expert?**
A: A vetted full-time marketer who works exclusively inside your team — your tools, your meetings, your goals — but is sourced and guaranteed by a partner like AnantaSutra. You pay just the salary plus a small transparent fee.

**Q: Are freelancers a good replacement for an agency?**
A: For scoped, one-off projects, yes. For ongoing marketing, freelancers split attention across clients and leave you doing the coordination; results vary widely.

**Q: Should an SME hire in-house instead of an agency?**
A: If you can afford the full cost (salary, benefits, tools, management) and can wait through a 40+ day hiring cycle, in-house gives maximum control. Embedded experts get you the same dedication in about 7 days without the hiring risk.
