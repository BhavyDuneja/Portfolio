## Why "without an agency" is the right instinct

I'll be blunt about the math. A typical multi-channel agency retainer runs $3,500–$5,000/month (₹3–4.5L) — and inside that retainer, your account is one of 15–30 the team juggles. You're not buying a marketer; you're buying slices of several people's attention, with 30–50% of your spend going to the agency's overhead and margin.

Meanwhile, a genuinely good dedicated marketer in India earns roughly ₹50,000–₹1,00,000/month at mid-level (₹6–12 LPA per published 2026 salary data — treat these as estimates, not verified offers). That's a full 160+ hours a month on *your* funnel, your CRM, your ads — not a retainer's leftover hours.

The question isn't whether to skip the agency. It's which non-agency route actually works for your stage.

## Step-by-step: hiring a dedicated marketer without an agency

### 1. Define the outcome, not the title
"Digital marketer" is not a role. Write one line: *"I need ₹X of revenue/leads from channel Y in Z days."* Awish Clinic came to us with exactly that framing — they went from Rs 25L to Rs 40L in monthly revenue in 60 days once a dedicated performance marketer owned the funnel end to end.

### 2. Pick specialist over generalist
The best independent marketers specialize. If 70%+ of your growth comes from paid ads, hire a performance marketer, not a "full-stack" one. Zoom Wheels grew 46% with a dedicated specialist focused on one motion done well; Royal Properties saw +38% the same way.

### 3. Decide who carries the hiring risk
This is the real fork in the road:
- **In-house:** you carry recruiting fees (often 20–25% of first-year salary), 3–6 months of ramp time, and full replacement risk.
- **Freelance:** you carry vetting, management, and the risk they deprioritize you for a bigger client.
- **Embedded:** the partner carries vetting and replacement. At AnantaSutra, if the expert isn't right, we replace them in 48 hours — that risk transfer is the whole point of the model.

### 4. Interview for ownership, not skills
Skills show up on a test. Ownership shows up in questions. Good dedicated marketers ask about your margins, your sales cycle, and your CRM before they ask about your ad budget. If they don't, keep looking.

### 5. Onboard like an employee, pay like a line item
Give them a company email, standup access, and a 30-day scorecard with 2–3 numbers. The dedication only materializes if you treat them as inside the team. That's the difference between "outsourced" and "embedded."

## What this costs vs. an agency (quick answer bullets)

- **Agency retainer:** ₹3–4.5L/month typical for multi-channel; your account shares team attention; 30–50% goes to overhead/margin.
- **Embedded dedicated marketer:** the marketer's salary (mid-level India: est. ₹50K–₹1L/month) + a small transparent fee. No markup, no lock-in.
- **In-house hire true cost:** salary + 20–30% benefits/overheads + recruiting fees + 3–6 months before full productivity.
- **Break-even logic:** if you spend more than ~₹1L/month on an agency and mostly need execution in 1–2 channels, a dedicated embedded marketer is usually cheaper *and* faster. (Estimate — run your own numbers.)

## When you should still use an agency

Honesty over pitch: if you need a one-off brand film, a website rebuild, or occasional bursts of many specialisms at once, a project agency can be right. Agencies also suit founders who want to be fully hands-off. But for continuous growth work — ads, SEO, CRM, content ops — dedication beats fragments of a team, every time we've measured it.
