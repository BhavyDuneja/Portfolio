## Start with the patients you already have

The cheapest patient to acquire is the one already in your database. Before spending a rupee on ads:

- **Recall systems.** Every patient who didn't book a follow-up is revenue sitting in your files. A simple WhatsApp/SMS recall sequence (7 days, 30 days, 90 days) is the single highest-ROI activity most clinics skip.
- **Treatment-plan follow-up.** Track every quoted-but-not-booked treatment plan and follow up within 48 hours, then weekly. Most clinics quote and forget.
- **Reviews on autopilot.** Ask every happy patient for a Google review at checkout — a printed QR code at reception plus a same-day WhatsApp link. Reviews are the #1 local ranking and trust signal, and they cost nothing.
- **Referral asks.** Patients refer when asked and when it's easy. A "bring a family member" offer or a simple referral card outperforms most ad campaigns we've seen for clinics.

Case in point: **Awish Clinic** went from Rs25L to Rs40L in monthly revenue in 60 days — and the biggest levers were exactly these unglamorous systems (recall, follow-up, review velocity, and tightly-run local campaigns), executed daily by one dedicated person rather than outsourced to an agency queue.

## Own your local search — it's where patients actually find you

Most patients pick a clinic from a Google search or Maps. You can own this without an agency:

- **Google Business Profile (GBP):** complete every field, add real photos monthly, post weekly, answer every review, and keep hours accurate. This is free and most competing clinics do it badly.
- **Location + service pages:** one page per core service ("root canal in [area]", "skin treatment in [area]") with real pricing guidance, FAQs, and doctor bios. Thin "we offer everything" pages don't rank.
- **Schema markup:** `MedicalClinic` / `Physician` / `FAQPage` JSON-LD so search engines and AI answer engines can parse who you are, where you are, and what you treat.
- **Speed and mobile:** patients book on phones. If your site takes 5 seconds to load, you're paying for clicks that bounce.

None of this needs an agency. It needs consistency — which is exactly what clinics struggle with, and why the "who does the work" question matters more than the tactics.

## The real problem isn't strategy — it's execution bandwidth

Every clinic owner knows they should do recall, reviews, GBP posts, and content. The gap is that nobody in the clinic owns it. The receptionist is busy, the doctor is treating, and "marketing" becomes whatever gets done in spare minutes.
