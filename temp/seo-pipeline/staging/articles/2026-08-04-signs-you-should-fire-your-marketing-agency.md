## 9 signs it's time to fire your agency

**1. Reports full of activity, empty of revenue.** If your monthly deck talks about impressions, reach, followers and "engagement" but can't tell you cost per lead or revenue attributed, that's not reporting — it's theatre. An agency that isn't in your CRM is guessing.

**2. The strategy hasn't changed in 6 months.** Same campaigns, same keywords, same creatives, quietly rebilled. Autopilot is the most expensive setting an agency has.

**3. You've never met the person doing the work.** You were sold by a senior strategist; your account is run by a junior handling 15 other clients. Ask directly: "Who touched my account this week, and for how many hours?" Silence is your answer.

**4. Every problem's solution is "increase the ad budget."** More spend hides bad strategy. A good marketer improves conversion before asking for more fuel.

**5. Missed deadlines have become normal.** One slipped launch is life. A pattern of rescheduled calls, delayed reports and late campaigns means your account isn't a priority.

**6. Your marketing looks like everyone else's.** If the work feels copy-pasted from a template used across their whole client list, it probably is.

**7. They never challenge you.** An agency that agrees with everything is billing you, not advising you.

**8. Account manager roulette.** Third "new point of contact" this year? Turnover on their side becomes ramp-up cost on yours — that you pay for.

**9. You're doing the project management.** If you chase, brief, remind and QA everything, you're paying agency fees for freelancer effort.

Count how many apply. Three or more, sustained over a quarter, and the accountability conversation is overdue. Five or more — stop negotiating and start transitioning.

## Why this keeps happening (it's the model, not the people)

Agencies aren't staffed by villains. The economics force this behaviour:

- **The markup problem.** Of every rupee (or dollar) you pay, 30–50% typically funds the agency's overhead — office, sales, management layers — before anyone does your work.
- **The attention problem.** Your account is one of many. The maths of retainers means junior people run day-to-day work while senior people sell.
- **The incentive problem.** Retainers reward retention, not results. Vanity metrics keep clients calm; revenue metrics invite hard questions.

You can switch agencies and inherit the same three problems with a new logo on the invoice.

## How to fire your agency cleanly (5 steps)

1. **Check your contract** — notice period, ownership of ad accounts, creatives and data.
2. **Secure your assets first** — admin access to ad accounts, analytics, domains, pixels, and social profiles *before* you give notice. This is the step people regret skipping.
3. **Export everything** — campaign history, audiences, negative keyword lists, reports.
4. **Give written notice** — short, professional, no debate.
5. **Overlap the replacement** — have your next resource (embedded expert or in-house) shadow the final 2 weeks so nothing drops.

## The 90-day test for whatever you choose next

Whoever runs your marketing next, hold them to this: by day 30, they're in your CRM and reporting cost per lead. By day 60, at least one experiment has been killed for underperforming (proof of active management). By day 90, revenue-linked metrics are trending, or there's a written diagnosis of why. Any partner who resists these terms is telling you who they are.
