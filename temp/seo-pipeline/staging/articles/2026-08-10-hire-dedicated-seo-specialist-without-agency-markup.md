## Why the agency model breaks for SEO specifically

SEO is a compounding, context-heavy discipline. The person doing it needs to know your product, your margins, your best-converting pages, and your sales conversations. Agencies structure against that:

- **Shared attention.** Your "SEO team" is typically one account manager and executives splitting time across many clients. You're buying hours, and you rarely know how many.
- **Markup on labor.** The person actually doing your keyword research and technical fixes is paid a fraction of your retainer. The rest is agency overhead and margin.
- **Reporting theater.** When attention is thin, output shifts toward what's easy to show (ranking screenshots, DA charts) instead of what moves revenue.
- **Context resets.** Account teams rotate. Every rotation, your business context walks out the door.

None of this means agencies are bad at SEO. It means the *structure* taxes you: you pay retail for wholesale labor, minus the focus.

## When each option actually wins

Direct answers, because this is where most advice gets mealy:

- **Choose an agency** if you need a short burst of multi-discipline work (migration + content + digital PR in 90 days) and have nobody internally to direct an SEO hire.
- **Choose a freelancer** for one-off audits, a technical fix list, or a defined project under ~₹1 lakh.
- **Choose a dedicated embedded specialist** if SEO is an ongoing growth channel for you — which it is for almost every business that gets meaningful organic traffic. Compounding channels need compounding context.
- **Choose a direct in-house hire** only if you have the recruiting muscle to vet SEO talent yourself and can absorb a bad-hire cycle. Most SMEs can't — which is exactly why the embedded model with a 48-hour replacement guarantee exists.
