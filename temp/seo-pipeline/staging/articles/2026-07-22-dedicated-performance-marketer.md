A dedicated performance marketer flips that. One person. Your accounts only. Full-time attention. This article breaks down what that costs, when it makes sense, and how to get one without hiring in-house.

## What does a dedicated performance marketer actually do?

A performance marketer is accountable for measurable outcomes — leads, purchases, cost per acquisition — not deliverables like "4 posts per week." A dedicated one, working solely for you, typically owns:

- **Media buying:** daily management of Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn, or whichever channels drive your revenue
- **Tracking and attribution:** conversion tracking, pixels, server-side events, so decisions run on real data
- **Creative testing:** structured A/B tests on hooks, formats, and landing pages
- **Budget allocation:** shifting spend to what's working, cutting what isn't — daily, not at the monthly review
- **Reporting you can act on:** CPA, ROAS, and pipeline numbers, not vanity dashboards

The key word is *dedicated*. Industry hiring pages describe this the same way we do: one marketer who "lives inside your campaigns every day" rather than checking in when the retainer hours allow.

## When a dedicated performance marketer is the right call

You're a fit if at least two of these are true:

- You spend (or plan to spend) enough on ads that a 15–20% efficiency gain pays for the person
- You've been burned by an agency assigning junior staff after the senior pitch
- You want daily optimization, not monthly check-ins
- You want the marketer inside your Slack/CRM/analytics — not emailing a contact form
- You need one or two channels done exceptionally well, not six channels done adequately

An agency still makes sense if you genuinely need tightly integrated execution across 6+ channels and have nobody internally to coordinate specialists. That's a real use case — it's just rarer than agencies suggest.
