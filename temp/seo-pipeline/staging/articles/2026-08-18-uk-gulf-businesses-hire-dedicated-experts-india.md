## The three routes (and who each is for)

### 1. Freelance marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal)

You hire directly, per project or per hour. Fast to start, zero commitment — and zero continuity. The freelancer juggles other clients, IP and payment terms are on you, and for UK firms there's IR35 status-determination exposure on longer engagements.

**Fits:** one-off projects under 4–6 weeks.

### 2. Your own Indian entity (or GCC/GIFT City structure)

Full control, best long-run unit economics at scale — but you're now running Indian payroll, PF/ESI/gratuity statutory costs (industry guides put these at 15–20% on top of gross salary), local HR and compliance. Setup takes months.

**Fits:** companies planning 20+ Indian heads.

### 3. The managed / embedded expert route

A partner recruits, legally employs and pays the expert in India; you direct their daily work like any team member. One contract, one invoice, no local entity. Guides for both the UK and UAE corridors call this the lowest-risk default — it removes IR35 exposure for UK clients and sidesteps the payroll problem for Gulf clients (paying an Indian employee directly in AED by wire is, as one 2026 guide puts it, "not legally clean").

**Fits:** SMEs that want one to ten dedicated experts, full-time, without building infrastructure.

## Time zones: the quiet advantage

- **Gulf ↔ India:** just 1.5 hours apart, year-round. A Dubai team starting 09:00 GST meets a Bangalore expert already online since 10:30 IST. Near-total overlap — the smallest delta of any major India hiring corridor after Singapore.
- **UK ↔ India:** 4.5–5.5 hours ahead. Overlap of roughly 8:00–13:00 UK time (12:30–17:30 IST) — a full half-day of live collaboration, plus India's morning as async "you wake up to finished work" time.

For collaboration-heavy roles, the Gulf corridor is effectively same-timezone. For UK firms, structure standups in the UK morning and you lose almost nothing.

## Compliance, in one paragraph per corridor

**UK:** the two things to get right are IR35 (for medium/large clients, *you* issue the Status Determination Statement, not the contractor) and GDPR data-processing terms. A managed model where the expert is legally employed in India under one UK contract removes the IR35 question entirely.

**Gulf:** there's no clean way to run INR payroll from a UAE entity, so almost every guide converges on an Employer-of-Record or managed staffing structure. The India–UAE CEPA has made the corridor administratively friendlier, but the employment must still sit with an Indian entity.
