## What is an embedded marketing team?

An embedded marketing team is external marketing talent that integrates directly into your company. They work in your Slack, join your standups, use your analytics, and answer to your leadership — the only difference from an employee is who runs their payroll.

Three things separate "embedded" from "agency":

1. **Exclusivity.** An embedded marketer works only on your business. An agency account manager might juggle 8–15 clients.
2. **Upstream involvement.** Embedded people sit in strategy conversations, not just execution handoffs. Ideas flow faster because there's no brief-and-wait cycle.
3. **Your direction, their employment.** You direct the work daily. The partner handles hiring, payroll, compliance, and replacement.

Quick-answer bullets:

- **Choose in-house** when you can afford a 60–90 day hiring cycle plus benefits, and marketing is core to your moat.
- **Choose embedded** when you need full-time, dedicated marketing capability now, without recruitment risk or agency markup.

## What does an embedded marketing team cost?

Real talk on numbers (labeled clearly — these are market estimates, not guarantees):

- **US freelance marketers:** roughly $50–100/hour; retainers of $1,000–3,000/month are common (industry estimates).
- **US full-time digital marketer:** around $62,000/year average salary, before benefits and payroll taxes (estimate from public salary data).
- **India, embedded model:** you pay the expert's actual salary — for a strong mid-level performance marketer that's typically Rs 40K–90K/month (estimate) — plus a small transparent fee. No 17–25% recruitment markups, no retainer padding.

The structural difference: agencies price on *value delivered to many clients at once*. Embedded pricing is *cost of one person working only for you*. For any business that needs more than ~20 hours a week of marketing work, embedded arithmetic usually wins.

## When an embedded marketing team is the wrong choice

I'll say this plainly because most vendors won't:

- If you need **less than ~15–20 hours/month** of marketing work, a freelancer is cheaper.
- If you need **six specialists for a 3-week launch**, an agency pod is genuinely better.
- If marketing **is your product** (e.g., you're a media company), build in-house and own the equity in that capability.

Embedded wins in the wide middle: SMEs and growth-stage companies that need serious full-time marketing but can't justify agency retainers or absorb hiring risk.
