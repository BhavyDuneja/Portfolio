## What a fractional marketing team actually is

"Fractional" means you buy a fraction of someone's week. A typical setup:

- A **fractional CMO** owns strategy, reporting, and budget decisions — 10–20 hrs/week
- Fractional **specialists** (paid media, content, SEO, automation) plug in underneath for a few hours each
- You pay monthly retainers per person, or one bundled fee

The pitch is real: you get senior judgment without a full-time executive salary. US sources put a full-time CMO's fully loaded cost at $400K–$650K+/year, so a $8K–$15K/month fractional retainer looks rational — sources claim 40–70% savings versus a full-time CMO hire. (Those are US-market figures from provider blogs, not audited data — treat as directional.)

## When a fractional marketing team is the right call

Be honest about fit. Go fractional if:

1. **You need board-level marketing judgment**, not hands on keyboards — positioning, pricing, channel strategy.
2. **Your revenue is roughly $2M–$50M** — the range multiple sources cite as the fractional sweet spot.
3. **You already have doers** (in-house juniors, an agency) and lack a conductor.
4. **The need is temporary** — a launch, a repositioning, a fundraise narrative.

## When a dedicated embedded expert beats a fractional marketing team

Choose a dedicated expert if the bottleneck is *doing*, not *deciding*. Most SMEs I meet don't have a strategy problem — they have an execution-consistency problem. Ads unmonitored for two weeks. Content calendars that die in week three. Leads that never get a follow-up sequence.

## The hybrid that actually works

Plenty of our clients run both: a fractional CMO (or the founder) sets direction one day a week, and a **dedicated embedded marketer executes full-time** underneath. You get senior judgment *and* daily throughput, usually for less than a single US fractional retainer.

If the strategy layer is already clear, skip the fractional layer entirely and put the budget into someone who ships every day.

## How to decide in 10 minutes

1. Write down last month's three biggest marketing failures.
2. If they're *direction* failures (wrong ICP, wrong channels) → fractional CMO.
3. If they're *execution* failures (inconsistency, slow follow-up, no one owning the funnel) → dedicated embedded expert.
4. If both → hybrid: light strategy layer + full-time doer.
5. Whatever you pick, demand transparency: hours, deliverables, and what happens if the person underperforms. If the provider can't answer "what's your replacement guarantee?" in one sentence, walk.

## FAQ

**What is a fractional marketing team?**
A group of senior part-time marketers, usually led by a fractional CMO, working a set number of hours per week for your business instead of full-time. You pay monthly retainers for a fraction of their capacity.

**How much does a fractional marketing team cost in 2026?**
Published US pricing puts fractional CMOs at $5,000–$20,000/month (up to $40,000 for large growth-stage companies) or $150–$500/hour. Full teams bundle CMO plus specialists into one larger retainer. These are provider-published figures, not audited benchmarks.

**Is a fractional marketing team better than an agency?**
Different jobs. Fractional leaders own strategy and business metrics; agencies execute channels. If you need a roadmap, go fractional. If you need one channel run, an agency can work — watch the 30–50% markup and pooled attention.

**What's the difference between a fractional marketer and a dedicated embedded expert?**

**Who should NOT hire a fractional marketing team?**
Businesses whose real problem is execution consistency. Part-time seniority can't compensate for having nobody doing the daily work — a full-time dedicated marketer will move the needle faster.
