# Research File 06 — Consultant / Independent Contractor Agreement

**Document purpose:** Engages an individual or firm as a non-employee service provider on a fixed-scope or time-and-material basis. Critically distinct from an Employment Agreement because of (i) IP-vesting differences under s.17 Copyright Act, (ii) absence of PF/ESI/gratuity, (iii) different tax treatment (TDS u/s 194J), (iv) no S&E Act / labour-code coverage, (v) different stamp duty / execution norms.

---

## 1. Overview & purpose

A Consultant Agreement is the right vehicle when Sutranet engages:

- **independent professionals** (architects, designers, technical consultants, lawyers, CAs) on retainer or project basis;
- **freelancers** (developers, writers, designers) for short engagements;
- **subject-matter experts** for advisory boards;
- **consulting firms** as sub-contractors under a flow-down from a Sutranet client SoW.

What it must not do:
- It must not create a **disguised employment relationship**. Indian PF/ESI authorities and courts apply substance-over-form tests (control test, integration test, economic-reality test) — *Workmen v. Greaves Cotton & Co.*, (1971) 2 SCC 658; *Hussainbhai v. Alath Factory Tezhilali Union*, (1978) 4 SCC 257; *Ram Singh v. Union Territory of Chandigarh*, (2004) 1 SCC 126. A misclassified "consultant" can trigger **back-PF liability for up to 7 years**, ESI exposure, and gratuity claims.

What it absolutely must contain:
- A **present-tense IP assignment** because **s.17(c) Copyright Act 1957 does not vest copyright in the engager when the author is not an employee**. Without an express assignment, Sutranet does not own deliverables created by consultants. This is the #1 due-diligence finding in Indian SME M&A.

## 2. Governing law & statutory basis

| Statute / Doctrine | Relevance |
|--------------------|-----------|
| **Indian Contract Act, 1872** | s.10, 23, 27, 28, 73, 74. Consultant Agreements are pure contracts — no statutory employment overlay. |
| **Specific Relief Act, 1963** | s.10 (specific performance default for commercial contracts), s.14 (limits — personal-service contract not enforceable; but deliverable-based contract can be), ss.36–42 (injunctions). |
| **Copyright Act, 1957** | **s.17 — proviso (b): work made for valuable consideration at instance of any person — first owner is that person.** Note: this proviso applies only to specific categories of works (paintings, engravings, photographs, cinematograph films) — not to general literary works including software. **For software, s.17(c) does not apply because the author is not an employee under "contract of service" — the consultant is on a "contract for services". Hence copyright remains with the consultant unless validly assigned under s.18 + s.19.** |
| **Patents Act, 1970** | s.6 — only inventor (or assignee from inventor) can apply. Consultant must assign or be named co-inventor. |
| **Trade Marks Act, 1999, Designs Act, 2000** | Assignment requirements. |
| **Information Technology Act, 2000** | s.43, 43A, 65, 72A — consultant access to data and systems. |
| **DPDP Act, 2023** | If consultant handles personal data, they may be a "Data Processor" under s.2(k); s.8(5) requires written contract; flow-down obligations needed. |
| **Income-tax Act, 1961** | s.194J (TDS on professional / technical services — 10%; 2% for "fees for technical services" if specifically classified); s.194C (works contract — 1%/2% for individual/company); s.195 (TDS on payment to non-residents); s.92 (transfer pricing for related-party); GST registration threshold of ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special states) for service providers. |
| **CGST Act, 2017** | GST on services, reverse-charge for unregistered supplier in some cases; place-of-supply rules; export of services zero-rated. |
| **EPF & MP Act, 1952 / ESI Act, 1948** | Risk of deemed-employee classification — must be guarded against by drafting and by operational practice. |
| **Indian Stamp Act, 1899 + state schedules** | Treated as "agreement not otherwise provided for"; nominal duty. |
| **Companies Act, 2013** | s.184 (interest disclosure), s.188 (related-party — if consultant is director/relative or holds > 2% stake). |
| **Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999** | Cross-border consultancy — TRC, FIRC, transfer-pricing. |

### Tests for distinguishing employee vs consultant (Indian courts)

1. **Control test** — does the engager control how the work is done (employee) or only what is to be delivered (consultant)?
2. **Integration test** — is the person integrated into the engager's organisation (employee) or operating their own business (consultant)?
3. **Economic-reality test** — does the person bear business risk, supply own equipment, work for multiple clients (consultant) or depend wholly on engager (employee)?
4. **Multiple-factor test** — modern approach; weighs all factors.

References: *Silver Jubilee Tailoring House v. Chief Inspector of Shops*, (1974) 3 SCC 498; *Ram Singh v. Union Territory of Chandigarh*, (2004) 1 SCC 126; *Sushilaben Indravadan Gandhi v. New India Assurance*, (2021) 7 SCC 151.

## 3. Mandatory & recommended clauses

### 3.1 Mandatory

1. **Parties** — Sutranet and Consultant (with PAN, GSTIN if registered, address, registered firm/LLP/company details if not individual).
2. **Recitals** — engagement on a contract-for-services basis; consultant is independent and not an employee.
3. **Scope of services** — fixed-scope (deliverables and milestones) or time-and-material (rate card, estimated hours, cap); SoW form attached.
4. **Term** — fixed term with renewal by written extension; or month-to-month with notice.
5. **Independent-contractor status** — express recital that consultant is independent; not entitled to employee benefits; bears own taxes; responsible for own PF/ESI/gratuity if applicable to consultant's own staff; no agency.
6. **Compensation** — fees (per deliverable, hourly, monthly retainer); invoicing schedule; payment terms (Net 15–30); GST extra; TDS at applicable rate.
7. **Out-of-pocket expenses** — pre-approval threshold; receipts.
8. **Intellectual property** — **present-tense assignment** of all Foreground IP, with all s.19 Copyright Act parameters (work, rights, duration entire term, territory worldwide, royalty included in fees), moral-rights waiver to maximum extent permissible, power of attorney for IP filings, further assurances.
9. **Background IP retention** — by consultant, with licence-back to Sutranet to the extent embedded in deliverables.
10. **Confidentiality** — full clause set; survival 3 years (perpetual for trade secrets); return / destruction.
11. **Personal data protection** — DPDP-aligned: if consultant processes personal data on Sutranet's instructions, full DPA-style clauses (purpose limitation, security, sub-processor approval, breach notification, audit, return/destruction); cross-border transfer compliance.
12. **No solicitation** — 12 months mutual; clients personally dealt with; employees consultant came in contact with.
13. **Warranties** — corporate authority; non-infringement; original work; no encumbrance; cleanliness from open-source copyleft contamination unless disclosed; compliance with laws; no conflict of interest.
14. **Indemnity** — IP infringement; confidentiality breach; DPDP breach; bodily injury / property damage at premises; tax misclassification (consultant indemnifies Sutranet against PF/ESI/gratuity claims by consultant or consultant's staff).
15. **Limitation of liability** — exclusion of indirect; cap on direct (typically fees paid in last 12 months); carve-outs (IP indemnity, confidentiality, DPDP, fraud, gross negligence, wilful misconduct, payment obligations, classification indemnity).
16. **Insurance** — professional indemnity (proportionate to engagement); cyber if handling data.
17. **Termination** — for convenience (15–30 days); for cause (material breach + 15-day cure); insolvency.
18. **Consequences of termination** — payment for work performed up to termination; deliverables in-progress with code/source files handed over; return of confidential information.
19. **Compliance with laws** — including ABAC, sanctions, modern slavery if applicable.
20. **Governing law and jurisdiction.**
21. **Dispute resolution** — escalation, then arbitration (sole arbitrator, seat at Bengaluru, A&C Act 1996); s.9 court relief preserved.
22. **Boilerplate** — notices, assignment (consultant cannot assign without consent; subcontracting prohibited or requires consent), entire agreement, severability, no waiver, counterparts, electronic execution.

### 3.2 Recommended

- **Conflict-of-interest disclosure and update obligation.**
- **Right to substitute key personnel** approval (relevant when consultant is a firm).
- **Audit rights** for time-and-material engagements (timesheet audit) and for DPDP compliance.
- **Background-check requirement** for consultant personnel accessing Sutranet/customer systems.
- **Tools and equipment** clarity — consultant supplies own (supports independent-contractor characterisation).
- **Place of work flexibility** — consultant determines (supports independent-contractor characterisation).
- **No exclusive engagement** — consultant may serve other clients (supports independent-contractor characterisation, unless explicit exclusivity is commercially necessary and time-bound).
- **Non-employment confirmation** — annual signed acknowledgement.

## 4. Drafting notes, pitfalls, enforceability tips

1. **The single most important clause is the present-tense IP assignment.** Use: "The Consultant hereby irrevocably assigns to the Company, with full title guarantee, all right, title and interest (including all intellectual property rights) in and to all work product, deliverables, inventions, discoveries, designs, developments, improvements, source code, object code, documentation, and all other materials, and all intellectual property rights therein, created or developed in the course of providing the Services (the 'Foreground IP'), throughout the world, for the entire duration of such rights, including all extensions and renewals, with the Consultant retaining no rights therein except as expressly licensed back under this Agreement." Do **not** use "agrees to assign" — see file 03 drafting note 1.

2. **Section 19 parameters** must all be present: identify the work (broad — "all work product"), specify the rights (all economic rights under Copyright Act / Patents Act / Designs Act / TM Act), duration (entire term), territorial extent (worldwide), royalty (treated as included in the fees). Without these, the assignment may be partly defective (e.g., territory defaults to India under s.19(6); duration defaults to 5 years under s.19(5)).

3. **Moral rights waiver** to the maximum extent permissible under s.57 Copyright Act 1957, with undertaking not to assert. Indian courts have not allowed full waiver of the right against distortion (*Amar Nath Sehgal v. UoI*, 2005), so don't promise more than the law allows.

4. **Avoid disguised employment.** Drafting alone is not enough — operational practice must match. Specifically:
   - Do **not** prescribe daily working hours or require attendance at office on fixed days.
   - Do **not** put the consultant in the org chart with reporting lines.
   - Do **not** issue Sutranet email / business cards (unless strictly necessary, in which case signature line says "Consultant to Sutranet").
   - Do **not** include consultants in employee benefits (insurance, leave, ESOP unless via separate Consultant Stock Plan).
   - Do **not** call performance reviews "appraisals".
   - **Do** invoice and pay against invoices; consultant raises invoices; not "salary".
   - **Do** require consultant to determine method of work; deliver outcome.

5. **Tax-classification indemnity.** Add an indemnity from consultant to Sutranet against any PF, ESI, gratuity, or income-tax claim arising from consultant's classification or from acts of consultant's own employees/sub-contractors. Without this, Sutranet absorbs the risk if a labour authority re-characterises.

6. **Sub-contracting prohibition.** Default: no sub-contracting without consent. If consent is given, consultant must flow-down all confidentiality, IP, and DPDP obligations to sub-contractors and remain primarily liable.

7. **Stamp duty.** Same as MSA — nominal in most states. ₹200 in Karnataka; ₹100–500 in Maharashtra. Always stamp; an unstamped consultant agreement is inadmissible until stamped + penalty.

8. **TDS at correct rate.** Consultant Agreement should expressly require consultant to provide PAN; specify the TDS rate that will apply (s.194J at 10% for professional/technical services; 2% for technical fees in some categories; s.194C for works contracts at 1%/2%); for non-residents, s.195 with TRC and Form 10F.

9. **GST.** Consultant should be registered if turnover crosses ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special states); EA should require GST invoice with GSTIN and HSN code; reverse-charge applicability flagged.

10. **DPDP Act compliance.** If the consultant accesses any personal data in Sutranet's possession, the consultant is a Data Processor under s.2(k). s.8(5) requires written contract. The Consultant Agreement must include the DPA-style obligations (see file 07).

11. **Background-IP register.** Many consultants bring proprietary frameworks, libraries, methodologies. The Agreement should require disclosure in a Schedule, treat them as Background IP, and licence-back any portions embedded in deliverables to Sutranet on a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free basis.

12. **Open-source.** Consultants disproportionately introduce open-source. Require disclosure of all third-party components in deliverables, with licences. Prohibit AGPL without prior approval.

13. **Insurance.** PII (professional-indemnity insurance) sized to engagement. For SoWs above ₹50 lakh, ₹1 crore PII is a reasonable ask.

14. **Post-termination cooperation.** Consultant must give a reasonable transition period for handover. Specify — e.g., up to 5 days at no additional cost; thereafter at agreed rates.

15. **Non-compete is void post-engagement.** Same s.27 ICA bar applies. Non-solicit is fine.

16. **Cross-border consultants.** Add (a) tax-residency certificate requirement; (b) PE (permanent establishment) protections — consultant warrants no PE created; (c) FEMA payment routing; (d) governing law and seat — Sutranet should resist foreign law/seat for SME consultancies; (e) sanctions / OFAC representations.

17. **Service mark: who can use whom's logo.** Default: neither without consent. Common exception: consultant may list Sutranet as a client in capability statements after written consent.

18. **Whistleblower / public-policy carve-outs** to confidentiality.

19. **Force majeure.** Consultant Agreements often omit FM — include it (network outage, regional internet shutdown, government lockdown).

20. **Survival clauses.** Confidentiality, IP, indemnity, limitation of liability, dispute resolution, governing law all survive termination.

## 7. Stamp duty & execution requirements

- **Stamp duty:** "Agreement not otherwise provided for" — Karnataka ₹200; Maharashtra ₹100–500; Delhi ₹50–100; Tamil Nadu ₹100. Use SHCIL e-stamp.
- **Registration:** Not required.
- **Patent / trade-mark / design assignment:** if IP assignment under the Consultant Agreement covers patents / TMs / designs, register the assignment with the appropriate IP Office (Form 16 Patents — within 6 months; TM-P TM Act — required to sue; Form 12 Designs).
- **Copyright registration:** optional but useful; ₹500 per work / ₹2,000 for software.
- **Execution:** Wet-ink with two witnesses recommended (especially because the IP assignment is essentially a deed) or Aadhaar e-Sign with audit trail. Counterparts; one copy each to consultant, project manager, and legal/HR repository.
- **For cross-border consultants:** apostille / notarisation may be needed for use abroad. PAN, GSTIN, TRC, Form 10F (for non-resident) collected upfront.

## 8. Cross-references to other Sutranet documents

- **MSA (file 04):** when consultants are sub-contracted to perform on a Sutranet client SoW, this Agreement provides the back-to-back flow-down. Check that confidentiality, IP, DPDP, indemnity, and liability cap align.
- **IPR Policy (file 03):** Background-IP / Foreground-IP definitions consistent; same Schedule format for Background-IP.
- **DPA (file 07):** DPDP annexure can be inline or attached; for sub-processor consultants, full DPA terms apply.
- **Employee NDA (file 02) and Employment Agreement (file 05):** parallel structure but s.17(c) Copyright Act vesting works for employees and not for consultants, so consultant assignment must be present-tense and explicit.
- **Client NDA (file 01):** confirms confidentiality flow-down — Sutranet's confidentiality obligations to its clients are mirrored in the Consultant Agreement.
